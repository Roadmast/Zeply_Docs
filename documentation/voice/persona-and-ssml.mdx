---
title: "Writing for voice"
description: "Write prompts that sound natural when an agent speaks them on a call."
---

On a call, everything the agent writes is read aloud. A reply that looks fine in chat can sound stiff, or confusing, when spoken. These habits make a voice agent easier to listen to.

## Keep turns short

Ask the agent to answer in one or two sentences, then stop and let the caller respond. People can't skim audio. A long answer with three options buries the first two.

```markdown
## Style
You are on a phone call. Answer in one or two short sentences.
Ask one question at a time. Never read out lists longer than three items.
```

## Write numbers and symbols the way you'd say them

Voices read what they're given. Tell the agent how to say the things your business mentions often:

- Prices: "fifteen hundred rupees", not "₹1,500/-"
- Times: "half past four", not "16:30"
- Phone numbers: read in small groups, with a pause between them
- Links and email addresses: avoid them on calls. Offer to send them on WhatsApp instead.

## No formatting

Markdown, bullet points, and emoji mean nothing in speech, and some voices read the symbols out loud. Sayvy AI already tells the model to speak naturally, but repeat it in your prompt if you see formatting slip into replies.

## Give it a personality, briefly

One or two lines on tone are enough: "Warm and patient. You speak like a helpful receptionist, not a salesperson." Long personality descriptions tend to make replies longer, not better.

## What isn't supported

SSML tags, such as `<break>` or `<prosody>`, are not supported. The voice would read them as text. To slow the agent down or add pauses, use shorter sentences and commas.
